# Beginner violin sequence brief — StandardTune

## Scope (not a path)

The tuner profile `violin` is **experimental**. StandardTune does **not** ship a Learn path for violin. This brief specifies **at most three** optional, short, **SELF-CHECK** lessons (`violin-*`) for a first-touch sequence. Do not insert them into acoustic, electric, classical, or bass curricula as required steps, unit gates, or progress prerequisites. Do not advertise a complete violin course.

**Sequencing standards (research only):** common Suzuki pre-piece and ABRSM Initial Grade starts place rest/play posture and bow hold before left-hand fingers, then open-string rhythms, then fingered notes. This brief stops at posture, bow, and open-string listening. Left-hand fingers are **out of scope** here.

**Instrument facts for copy:**

- Violin has **no frets**. Do not teach guitar tab, fret numbers, or six-string guitar maps.
- Open strings (low → high): **G3, D4, A4, E5** (`violin-standard`).
- The app tuner can check **open-string pitch only**. It does **not** grade bow direction, bow distribution, contact point, or intonation of fingered notes. Say that plainly in every lesson that mentions the microphone or tuner.
- Do **not** name or arrange a copyrighted method-book piece (including the well-known nursery theme used in many violin books) as our exercise. Use original open-string rhythm and listening tasks only.

**Assessment:** SELF-CHECK prose and ear/hand observation only. No scored follow-me drill, no fake fingered-note matching, no claim that a green tuner needle proves bow technique.

## Lesson `violin-rest-play`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `violin-rest-play` |
| **title** | Rest Position and Playing Position |
| **description** | Move between a quiet rest hold and a balanced playing posture before any bowing. |
| **minutes** | 5 |
| **outcome** | Switch calmly from rest position to playing position with a stable instrument and relaxed shoulders. |
| **practice** | Stand or sit tall. Hold the violin in rest position at your side or across the chest as your teacher prefers. Raise it to the shoulder/collarbone for playing position, place the jaw lightly, then return to rest. Repeat several times without rushing. Do not start the bow yet. |
| **readyWhen** | The violin feels balanced in playing position when the left hand is not gripping the neck, and your shoulders stay soft when you return to rest. |

### Sections

#### Rest first, then raise

Beginners often tense the neck and left hand before a single open string sounds. Start in rest position: the violin is quiet against your body, feet planted, shoulders down. Count a slow breath, then lift the instrument to playing position so the lower bout rests on the collarbone or shoulder pad and the jaw contacts the chinrest lightly—enough to steady the instrument, not enough to clamp. Keep the left hand free of supporting duty for a moment: if the violin tips when you release the neck, adjust the shoulder or jaw before adding any finger pressure. This self-check is about balance and comfort; StandardTune does not grade posture, and the experimental violin tuner is not used in this lesson.

#### Soft shoulders, no frets to find

Violinists do not press behind fret wires. There is nothing to “locate” on a grid today—only a calm frame for later bowing. After three or four rest-to-play cycles, pause in playing position and notice whether the left wrist is forced or the right arm is already lifting as if to bow. Lower the bow arm and return to rest once more. Stop if anything hurts; a short posture session is never a reason to push through pain. Mark the lesson practised when *you* feel a repeatable, unhurried switch between rest and play—not when any app score appears.

## Lesson `violin-bow-open`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `violin-bow-open` |
| **title** | Bow Hold and Open-String Quarters |
| **description** | Settle a soft bow hold, then play even quarter notes on one open string. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Hold the bow without a clenched fist and play several steady open-string quarter notes on one chosen string. |
| **practice** | Form a rounded bow hold (thumb opposite the stick, fingers soft). On open D or open A only, count 1-2-3-4 at a slow pulse and draw one bow stroke per beat. Stay on open strings; leave left-hand fingers off the fingerboard. Listen for even starts—not for speed. |
| **readyWhen** | You can play four calm quarter notes on one open string while counting aloud, with a soft bow hand and no need to grip the stick hard. |

### Sections

#### A soft hold before the stroke

The bow is a flexible stick and hair, not a pick or a fretboard map. Curve the fingers over the stick so the thumb meets it opposite the hand, then release any squeeze until the hold still feels secure when you lift the bow a few centimetres. Keep the wrist free enough to flex later; do not lock it straight. Place the violin in playing position from the previous lesson, rest the bow hair on one open middle string (D or A is a comfortable first choice), and feel the weight of the stick without pressing the hair flat into the string. Guitar tab and fret numbers do not apply—there is no fret to “find” under a left-hand finger for this exercise. StandardTune’s experimental violin profile may show pitch if you open the tuner, but it does **not** grade bow direction, bow length, or which open string you chose by technique alone.

#### Open-string quarters, one pulse

Choose one open string and stay there for the whole session. At a slow count of four, move the bow on each beat so each stroke lasts about one quarter note. Alternate direction if that feels natural (down then up), but do not treat direction changes as something the app can score—**the app does not grade bow direction**. Aim for a clear start and a quiet stop at the end of each count rather than a long, uncontrolled scrape. If the tone scratches, lighten the arm weight or shorten the stroke before pressing harder. Leave all left-hand fingers floating above or away from the string; fingered intonation is out of scope and **the app does not grade intonation of fingered notes**. This is a listening self-check: even volume and a steady pulse that *you* hear, not a microphone pass on bow craft.

## Lesson `violin-open-listen`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `violin-open-listen` |
| **title** | Open String High or Low |
| **description** | Use the violin tuner to hear whether an open string sits sharp or flat of its target. |
| **minutes** | 5 |
| **outcome** | Name whether a chosen open string needs to rise or fall to match G3, D4, A4, or E5. |
| **practice** | Open Tune, select the experimental violin profile and standard G–D–A–E. Bow one open string gently. Watch whether the reading is high or low of the selected target, then turn the peg or fine tuner in small steps. Recheck after each tiny change. |
| **readyWhen** | You can say “too high” or “too low” for the open string you are adjusting and bring it nearer the target with small moves—not one large twist. |

### Sections

#### One open string against the needle

Select the violin instrument in Tune (it may still be labelled experimental) and the standard open targets G3, D4, A4, and E5. Bow a single open string with a short, even stroke so the pitch can settle. Compare what you hear with the on-screen target: if the needle or cents reading sits above the goal, the string is high and needs to come down; if it sits below, the string is low and needs to rise. Work in tiny peg or fine-tuner moves, then bow again. Do not expect the app to judge bow direction while you tune—**the app does not grade bow direction**. Use only open strings; pressing fingers on the fingerboard creates pitches the beginner sequence is not teaching yet, and **the app does not grade intonation of fingered notes**.

#### High, low, and when to stop

After you can label high versus low on one string, check the other three open strings the same way, still one at a time. Keep volume comfortable and the room as quiet as you can; noise can make an experimental profile jump. If a reading flickers, mute other strings lightly with a resting finger or cloth touch on the unused strings, then bow again. Stop while the open pitch is close and stable rather than chasing a perfect freeze. Mark the lesson practised when *you* can explain whether the open string should go up or down and make a small correction—self-check mastery, not a claim that Learn now offers a full violin path.
