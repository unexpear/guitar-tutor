# Beginner mandolin sequence (StandardTune) — self-check only

## Scope (not a path)

Tuner profile `mandolin` is **experimental**. Mandolin is **not** a Learn instrument today (`LEARNING_INSTRUMENTS` is guitar + bass only). This brief specifies **at most three** beginner lessons (`mandolin-*`). They are **not** a lesson path, **must not** be wired into acoustic/electric/classical/bass curricula, and **must not** reuse guitar chord boxes, fourths-tuned string maps, or guitar drills.

**Instrument facts for copy:**

| Fact | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Standard G | Courses tuned in **fifths**: G3, D4, A4, E5 (thickest → thinnest) |
| Courses | Each pitch is a **doubled course** (two unison strings): G–G, D–D, A–A, E–E |
| Pick | Strike **both** strings of a course so they speak together |
| Later skills (out of scope) | Two-finger chords, simple tremolo, and chop come **after** these three lessons—not guitar shapes in fourths |

**Scoring limit:** StandardTune cannot pitch-score mandolin courses today (paired unisons / course maps are unsupported by the matcher). Every lesson below is **SELF-CHECK only**: ear, tuner needle while tuning, and hand observation. Do **not** attach a fake mono/poly drill, guitar-string index targets, or a claim that a green pitch score proves a clean course.

**Copyright:** Use original exercises only. No named fiddle tunes, no transcribed melodies from recordings, no copyrighted song hooks.

**Prerequisite:** A playable mandolin in (or near) standard G, a pick, and access to Tune with the mandolin profile selected. No guitar-path completions are required.

## Lesson `mandolin-tune-courses`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `mandolin-tune-courses` |
| **title** | Tune the Four Courses |
| **description** | Meet standard G as four doubled courses (G3 D4 A4 E5), and tune each pair with the experimental mandolin profile—self-check, no scored drill. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Name the four courses low to high and bring each unison pair close enough that both strings of a course sound the same pitch. |
| **practice** | Open Tune, choose Mandolin / Standard G (expect an experimental-profile note if shown). From the thickest course, select G, pluck **both** G strings together, then check each G string alone if they disagree. Repeat for D, A, and E. Mute neighbouring courses between attempts. Judge by ear and the tuner—not a lesson score. |
| **readyWhen** | You can say G–D–A–E in order, and each course’s two strings sound matched (or you know which peg still needs a tiny turn). |

### Sections

#### Courses, not six separate guitar strings

A mandolin in standard G is tuned in perfect fifths: sounding G3, D4, A4, and E5 from the thickest course to the thinnest. Unlike a guitar in fourths, you do not count six independent string letters for everyday playing—you count **four courses**, and each course is two strings tuned to the **same** pitch. When you “play the G course,” both G strings should ring together as one voice. Sit so the instrument is stable without gripping the neck. Use the mandolin tuner profile even though it is experimental: it aims the reference frequencies at mandolin range, but room noise and paired strings can still confuse a phone microphone. If the needle jumps, mute every other course, pluck once, and listen before turning a peg.

#### Match the pair, then move on

Work thickest to thinnest. Select the course label in Tune, strike both strings of that course with one pick stroke, and listen for beats or a thin edge that means the two unisons disagree. If they disagree, mute one string of the pair lightly and tune the other to the display, then bring its partner to match by ear. Make tiny peg turns; stop if you are unsure which machine belongs to which string. After all four courses, circle once more—tuning one pair can tug another. Mark this lesson practised when **you** can name G, D, A, and E and hear matched unisons; StandardTune will not certify courses with a pitch-scored lesson drill.

## Lesson `mandolin-clean-course`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `mandolin-clean-course` |
| **title** | One Clean Open Course |
| **description** | Pick through both strings of a single open course for a clear, even sound—self-check only. |
| **minutes** | 5 |
| **outcome** | Produce several clear open-course strokes where both strings of the chosen course speak together and neighbours stay quiet. |
| **practice** | Choose one open course (D or A is a comfortable middle start). With a short pick tip, stroke **through both strings** of that course only. Listen for a single blended pitch, not one loud string and one ghost. Mute the other three courses with spare fingers or a light touch. Repeat slowly; do not ask the app to score the note. |
| **readyWhen** | You hear both strings of your chosen course on most strokes, without a sustained drone from another course. |

### Sections

#### Pick through the pair on purpose

Hold the pick so a small tip shows past thumb and index. Aim the stroke so the tip travels through **both** strings of one course in a single motion—treating them as one target, not as two guitar strings to catch separately. Start on an open middle course (D or A) so the motion is easy to see and hear. Keep the wrist small; a large swing makes it easy to clip a neighbour course. If only one string of the pair speaks, adjust the pick angle or tip length rather than pressing harder with the fretting hand (which should stay released for open courses). The goal is a blended unison “course” sound, the habit every later tremolo or chop will need.

#### Silence is part of a clean stroke

After each stroke, listen for leftover ring on courses you did not intend to play. Lightly touch unused courses so they cannot sustain. Count a short rest between strokes and ask whether the sound stopped when you wanted it to. This is a listening self-check: the microphone may report a pitch near D4 or A4, but it cannot prove you struck both strings of the course or muted the rest. Do not open a guitar fretting drill or a single-string bass target and pretend it maps to a mandolin course. Ready means **your** ear hears a clear paired course and quiet neighbours—not a green lesson score.

## Lesson `mandolin-open-fifth`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `mandolin-open-fifth` |
| **title** | Two-Note Fifth, One Fretted Course |
| **description** | Sound a perfect fifth across two courses, then fret both strings of one course cleanly—self-check only; no fiddle tunes. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Play a clear two-pitch open fifth (G–D, D–A, or A–E) with each course’s unison pair speaking, and place one finger on one course without losing that fifth when you return to open courses. |
| **practice** | Pick open G (both strings), then open D (both strings), then both together as a G–D fifth. Try D–A and A–E the same way. Next, fret **only one course** (both unisons under one fingertip) at fret 2, pick that course alone, release, and return to an open fifth. Mute unused courses. Judge by ear—no scored drill; no song melody. |
| **readyWhen** | You can hold a calm open fifth with both strings of each course speaking, and you can fret one course then return to the open fifth without a neighbour-course drone. |

### Sections

#### Hear the fifth between courses

Adjacent open courses on a mandolin are a perfect fifth apart: G to D, D to A, and A to E. That is the opposite layout from a guitar’s fourths between most strings, so do not import guitar power-chord shapes or six-string diagrams. Pick the lower open course through both strings, then the higher open course through both strings, then sound them together as a two-pitch fifth. Listen for two blended course voices, not four separate thin scrapes. Mute the unused courses. This exercise is original interval practice—not a fiddle tune, reel, or song melody. If a phone tuner flickers while two courses ring, trust your ear for the interval and use Tune only to re-check single courses when needed.

#### Fret one course, keep course thinking

Add one fretting-hand job without switching to guitar chord boxes: press **both strings of a single course** with one fingertip just behind a fret (start at fret 2 on a middle course), pick only that course, then release and return to an open fifth you already like (for example open G with open D). The fretted stroke trains one-course pressure; the return checks that you still hear a clean fifth across two courses. Later lessons—not these three—can build two-finger chords, a simple tremolo on one course, or a chop. For now, mark ready when **you** hear clear open fifths and a controlled one-course fret, knowing the app cannot pitch-score mandolin courses yet.
