# Beginner bridges: baritone, 7-, 8-, and 12-string guitar (StandardTune)

## Scope (not new syllabi)

Tuner profiles `guitar-baritone`, `guitar-7`, `guitar-8`, and `guitar-12` already exist. Learn already ships **six-string** acoustic and electric beginner paths on standard E–A–D–G–B–e. This brief specifies **at most four** optional, short, **SELF-CHECK** lessons—one per instrument—that bridge a learner who already finished six-string foundations onto those tuner-only shapes.

These four lessons are **not** new beginner syllabi, **not** full extended-range curricula, and **must not** be inserted into the acoustic or electric six-string paths as required steps, unit gates, or progress prerequisites. They may appear only as optional extras (or stay out of the catalogue until product chooses to surface them).

**Prerequisite (every lesson):** finish the six-string path first (acoustic or electric). Do not start here as a first instrument course. StandardTune’s scored guitar drills, chord boxes, and song charts remain six-string maps; do not invent a new chord catalogue, scored extra-string targets, or copyrighted riffs for these bridges.

**Scoring limit:** treat every lesson as **SELF-CHECK prose**—ear, fretting hand, and muting observation. Do not attach a fake microphone drill that pretends the matcher scores B1, F♯1, octave courses, or muting quality.

**Tunings (reference only):**

| Profile | Role | Open strings (low → high) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six-string (existing paths) | Foundation | E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 |
| `guitar-baritone` | B-standard **range** (same intervals, lower) | B1 E2 A2 D3 F♯3 B3 |
| `guitar-7` | Six-string set **plus low B** to mute or use | B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 |
| `guitar-8` | Seven-string set **plus low F♯** to mute or use | F♯1 B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 |
| `guitar-12` | Six **paired courses** (octave/unison), not twelve independent melody strings | E2/E3 A2/A3 D3/D4 G3/G4 B3/B3 E4/E4 |

Baritone is about hearing and fretting in **B-standard range**. Seven- and eight-string bridges teach **keeping added low strings quiet** when the part lives on the familiar six. Twelve-string is paired courses, a **lighter touch**, **unstable tuning**, and a **poor first instrument**—use it only after six-string comfort.

## Lesson `guitar-baritone-range`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `guitar-baritone-range` |
| **title** | Baritone Range in B Standard |
| **description** | After the six-string path, retune to baritone B standard and confirm the same relative layout sits a fourth lower. Self-check only—no new chord catalogue. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Name the six open baritone pitches (B–E–A–D–F♯–B) and play one open and one fretted note clearly in that lower range. |
| **practice** | Finish the six-string path first. In Tune, select baritone B standard. Pluck each open string once thickest to thinnest, then fret a familiar shape (for example low-string fret 3) and listen for a clear note below six-string pitch. Judge tone yourself—the app does not score a separate baritone syllabus. |
| **readyWhen** | You can say the open string names in order and hear that the whole set sits lower than standard six-string E, without needing a new set of chord boxes. |

### Sections

#### Same layout, lower ceiling

Baritone B standard keeps the familiar fourths-and-third guitar spacing, but every open string sounds a perfect fourth below six-string E standard: B1 E2 A2 D3 F♯3 B3. Finish the six-string acoustic or electric path first so fretting, diagrams, and pulse already make sense; this bridge only relocates that map. Sit as you usually do. Choose the baritone tuner profile, pluck the thickest string once, and compare it to the E you remember from six-string work—it should feel like the same “bottom string” job at a darker pitch. Work across the set without inventing new shapes: any open-chord diagram you already know still describes relative string numbers, but the sounding keys are lower. Do not treat this as a fresh beginner syllabus or a reason to load a second chord catalogue.

#### One fretted note in the real range

Pick one simple fretted target you already trust on six-string—such as fret 3 on the lowest string—and play it slowly on the baritone. Listen for a clean start, then release. If the phone or amp emphasises an overtone, mute the idle strings and try again closer to the body. The goal is comfort in B-standard range, not speed or a riff. Mark the lesson practised when *you* can name the opens and hear one solid fretted note in that lower register. Pitch detectors may help you tune; they do not certify baritone technique or replace finishing the six-string path first.

## Lesson `guitar-7-mute`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `guitar-7-mute` |
| **title** | Quiet the Low B on Seven |
| **description** | After the six-string path, find open low B on a seven-string, then keep that string silent while you play familiar notes on the upper six. Self-check only—no new chords. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Identify open B1 next to open E2, and deliberately mute the seventh string while the six-string set rings cleanly. |
| **practice** | Finish the six-string path first. In Tune, select seven-string B standard. Pluck open B1 once and compare it to open E2. Then play a short open E–A–D–G–B–e sequence (or one familiar open shape) while resting a fretting fingertip or thumb so B1 stays silent. Judge the silence yourself. |
| **readyWhen** | You can tell open B is lower than open E, and a short phrase on the upper six does not leave a low-B drone. |

### Sections

#### Find the extra low string, then park it

Seven-string standard adds one thicker string tuned to B1 beneath the E2 you already use on six-string guitars. Finish the six-string path first; the upper six strings are the same E–A–D–G–B–e map you already practised. Choose the seven-string tuner profile, pluck the lowest string once, and listen for a pitch clearly below open E. That landmark is enough for day one—do not build a new chord catalogue around seven-note grips. When the part would have lived on six strings, the seventh string’s first musical job is often silence, not a new bass line.

#### Mute on purpose while the six stay clear

Play a slow sequence on the familiar six strings only. Before each attack, check that B1 is stopped: a light fretting-hand touch across the lowest string, or a resting thumb, is enough. Count a short rest and listen for a leftover low hum. If B sings along, mute, breathe, and restart that beat. This is a listening self-check. StandardTune’s six-string drills and chord boxes do not score B1 or certify muting; mark ready when *your* ears hear the upper six without a B drone. No copyrighted riffs—use open strings or shapes you already know from the six-string path.

## Lesson `guitar-8-mute`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `guitar-8-mute` |
| **title** | Quiet F♯ and B on Eight |
| **description** | After the six-string path (and ideally the seven-string mute habit), find open F♯1 and B1 on an eight-string, then keep both low strings silent while the upper six speak. Self-check only. |
| **minutes** | 6 |
| **outcome** | Identify open F♯1 and B1 by ear below the six-string set, and mute both while playing clear notes on E–A–D–G–B–e. |
| **practice** | Finish the six-string path first. In Tune, select eight-string F♯ standard. Pluck open F♯1, then B1, comparing each to open E2. Play a short phrase on the upper six while both low strings stay dead. Optional: sound each low open once to confirm the landmark, mute fully, then resume. Judge ringing yourself. |
| **readyWhen** | You can name F♯ and B as the two strings below E, and a familiar six-string phrase does not leave a low F♯ or B cloud. |

### Sections

#### Two outer landmarks under the six you know

Eight-string F♯ standard keeps seven-string B standard and adds one thicker string at F♯1. Finish the six-string path first so the E–A–D–G–B–e core is already fluent; this bridge only adds quiet neighbours. Use the eight-string tuner profile. Pluck F♯1 once, then B1, then open E2, and listen for a stepped climb into the range you already trust. Keep volume comfortable—low strings can boom and confuse a phone mic. Do not open a new chord catalogue or scored eight-string drill; the beginner skill is orientation plus silence, not a second syllabus.

#### Mute both lows while the phrase stays on six

When the part lives on the familiar six, F♯1 and B1 are cargo until you choose them. Play a slow open or fretted phrase on E–A–D–G–B–e only. Between notes, rest fretting fingertips or the side of the picking hand so both low strings stay dead. Listen for a muddy after-ring or sympathetic growl. If either low string speaks, mute both, reset, and replay that beat. Optional self-check: pluck F♯ and B once each to confirm landmarks, silence them fully, then return to the six-string phrase. Ready means *you* hear a clean upper set—not a detector claiming it scored unsupported low strings. No copyrighted riffs; reuse six-string material you already earned.

## Lesson `guitar-12-courses`

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **id** | `guitar-12-courses` |
| **title** | Twelve-String Courses and Light Touch |
| **description** | After the six-string path, treat a twelve-string as six paired courses: lighter attack, frequent retuning, and a poor first instrument. Self-check only—no new chord catalogue. |
| **minutes** | 7 |
| **outcome** | Explain that each “string” is a course of two, play one open course with a light touch, and retune at least one drifted pair without forcing the neck. |
| **practice** | Finish the six-string path first. In Tune, select twelve-string standard. Pluck one course (both wires of a pair) gently and listen for a chorus, not a single wire. Check tuning on that course, then on its neighbour; expect drift. Strum one familiar open shape softly once. Stop if the instrument fights you—twelve-string is optional colour, not a beginner starter. |
| **readyWhen** | You can say courses come in pairs, keep the attack light enough that both wires speak without harsh rattle, and you know to retune often rather than muscle through. |

### Sections

#### Paired courses, not twelve solo strings

A twelve-string is six courses: four octave pairs and two unison pairs on the top, matching the six-string order you already learned. Finish the six-string path first; diagrams still count six courses, not a new twelve-note chord vocabulary. Choose the twelve-string tuner profile and pluck one course so both wires move together. Listen for a bright, doubled sound rather than hunting twelve independent melody lines. Because doubled strings pull hard and go out quickly, unstable tuning is normal—expect to check the tuner again after a few minutes of playing. Treat this instrument as a later colour after six-string comfort, not as someone’s first guitar.

#### Lighter touch, then retune without a fight

Use less pick or thumb than you would on a six-string dreadnought. A heavy attack fights the octave wire, buzzes the nut, and knocks courses out faster. Strum or brush one familiar open shape once at low volume; listen for both wires of each course you intend to hear. If a course is sour, mute the others, select that course in Tune, and make tiny peg changes—do not keep cranking an uncertain string. Mark the lesson practised when *you* feel the lighter touch, hear paired courses, and accept frequent retuning as part of the instrument. No new chord catalogue and no copyrighted riffs; soft open shapes from the six-string path are enough. A twelve-string remains a poor first instrument—keep learners on six-string foundations before this bridge.
